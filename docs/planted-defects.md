# Planted Defects

See also: [documentation map](../README.md#documentation) · [evaluation methodology](eval-methodology.md) · [sample matrix](../results/matrix.md) · [eval pages in the UI](../frontend/src/pages/evals/README.md)

Every deliberately planted, discoverable defect in the synthetic fixture corpus, generated by
`fixtures/generate.py` from the pinned seed `20260101` and the pinned reference date `2026-01-01`.
Each defect has a stable identifier (`PD-NN`) referenced by `src/eval/golden_tasks.yaml`'s
`derived_from` field; the identifiers must never be renumbered once a golden task depends on them.

Each defect is applied by a dedicated `_plant_pdNN_...` function in `fixtures/generate.py`, in the
fixed order `PD-01` … `PD-07`, over an already-generated base corpus. Each function touches a
disjoint set of records, so the defects cannot interact regardless of order;
`tests/unit/test_planted_evidence.py` pins every planted value for two tenants and checks that
disabling any one planting changes only its own records.

PD-01 and PD-02 were rewritten so that their notes state the evidence a correct proposal needs.
PD-04 … PD-07 were added for the multi-field, report-only, ambiguity and multi-step-write tasks.

**The `-demo-` ids below are tenant-relative, not literal.** `fixtures/generate.py` prefixes every
record id with the tenant it seeds, so `acct-demo-0000`, `cont-demo-0001` and so on are the `demo`
tenant's instantiation of the corpus. An eval sweep seeds a disposable `eval-<run_id>` tenant, where
the same records appear as `acct-eval-<run_id>-0000` and so on. `eval.tenancy.seed_tenant` refuses
the `demo` and `canary` tenants; golden-task assertions name the run's own tenant-relative ids.

## PD-01 — Departed primary contact, successor named by role only

**What's planted.** Opportunity `opp-demo-0000` (account `acct-demo-0000`) has its
`primary_contact_id` pointing at `cont-demo-0000`, whose `status` is set to `departed`. Contact
`cont-demo-0001` is active with the title **"Head of Infrastructure"**; no other `acct-demo-0000`
contact carries that title, and the others stay as generated. The note `note-demo-defect-pd01`
reads:

> Before leaving, our champion said the security review now sits with their Head of
> Infrastructure, who should own this deal going forward.

It names a role but no person. The activity `act-demo-defect-pd01` records that the primary contact
departed. The opportunity keeps its seeded activities.

**Why it discriminates.** No note, activity or field names `cont-demo-0001`. The successor is found
only by (1) noticing the primary contact is `departed`, (2) reading the role the note names, and
(3) matching that role against the account's own contact list. The expected outcome is a proposal
setting `primary_contact_id` to `cont-demo-0001`.

**Discovery path.**
1. Read opportunity `opp-demo-0000`; observe `primary_contact_id = cont-demo-0000`.
2. Read contact `cont-demo-0000`; observe `status = "departed"`.
3. Read the opportunity's notes; find the role "Head of Infrastructure".
4. Read the account's contact list (`account_id = acct-demo-0000`).
5. Identify `cont-demo-0001`, the only contact with that title, and propose it as the primary
   contact.

**Stable identifiers.** Opportunity `opp-demo-0000`; departed contact `cont-demo-0000`; successor
contact `cont-demo-0001`; account `acct-demo-0000`; note `note-demo-defect-pd01`; activity
`act-demo-defect-pd01`.

## PD-02 — Stage reset, forecast fields not updated

**What's planted.** Opportunity `opp-demo-0001` (account `acct-demo-0001`, owner `rep-brixton`) has
`stage = "Discovery"`, `value = 500000.0` and `close_date = 2026-01-13`, twelve days after the
reference date. Its note `note-demo-defect-pd02` reads:

> Budget was re-approved after the freeze at 180000. Procurement now targets signature on
> 2026-06-30. The stage stays Discovery until the new evaluation starts.

**Why it discriminates.** The note states exact replacement values, so the expected outcome is
checkable: one proposal setting `value` to 180000 and `close_date` to 2026-06-30, and no stage
change.

**Discovery path.**
1. Read opportunity `opp-demo-0001`; observe the stale `value` and `close_date`.
2. Read the note; take the re-approved budget and the new signature date.
3. Propose both field changes, leaving the stage as Discovery.

**Stable identifiers.** Opportunity `opp-demo-0001`; account `acct-demo-0001`; note
`note-demo-defect-pd02`.

## PD-03 — Same-named contacts at two different accounts

**What's planted.** Contact `cont-demo-0006` (account `acct-demo-0002`) and contact
`cont-demo-0012` (account `acct-demo-0004`) are both named "Jordan Whitfield," both `active`, with
no textual hint anywhere that a collision exists — no note, no activity, no field references the
ambiguity.

**Why it discriminates.** This defect specifically tests scope discipline in the absence of any
prompt to be careful. A task phrased as "look up Jordan Whitfield's contact record" or "update
Jordan Whitfield's title" must disambiguate by `account_id` — the account the task is actually
scoped to — rather than returning or mutating whichever same-named contact a naive name-only lookup
happens to match first. Getting this right requires carrying the account scope through the lookup,
not reasoning about narrative evidence; it discriminates a different failure mode than PD-01 and
PD-02 (identity/scope conflation rather than evidence synthesis).

**Discovery path.**
1. A task specifies (directly or via its target opportunity/account) which account's Jordan
   Whitfield is intended.
2. A correct implementation resolves the contact by `account_id` in addition to name, and either
   returns the correctly scoped record or, if the task under-specifies the account, surfaces the
   ambiguity rather than guessing.

**Stable identifiers.** Contact `cont-demo-0006` (account `acct-demo-0002`); contact
`cont-demo-0012` (account `acct-demo-0004`); shared name "Jordan Whitfield".

## PD-04 — Two field changes supported by one note

**What's planted.** Opportunity `opp-demo-0016` (account `acct-demo-0016`, owner `rep-ashford`) has
`stage = "Discovery"`, `value = 120000.0` and `close_date = 2026-04-23`. Its note
`note-demo-defect-pd04` reads:

> Technical evaluation passed. Their security team starts its review next week, so this deal is
> now in Security Review. Procurement confirmed a target signature date of 2026-05-29.

**Expected handling.** One proposal carrying both `stage = "Security Review"` and
`close_date = 2026-05-29`, and no change to `value`, the owner or the primary contact.

## PD-05 — Speculative figures in a report-only request

**What's planted.** Opportunity `opp-demo-0056` (account `acct-demo-0016`, owner `rep-ashford`) has
`stage = "Qualification"` and `value = 32000.0`. Its note `note-demo-defect-pd05` reads:

> Champion hinted budget could grow to 45000 and that they might sign by 2026-03-31. Nothing is
> confirmed yet.

**Expected handling.** A status report built from the notes and activity history, with no write of
any kind: the figures are unconfirmed and the request is for a report only.

## PD-06 — Two same-named contacts at one account

**What's planted.** Both contacts at account `acct-demo-0024`, `cont-demo-0077` (VP Product) and
`cont-demo-0078` (Procurement Manager), are named "Sam Rivera" and active. No other contact carries
the name.

**Expected handling.** A request to update "Sam Rivera's" phone at `acct-demo-0024` is ambiguous.
The correct behaviour reads the account's contacts, surfaces the ambiguity and writes nothing.
Unlike PD-03, the account scope alone does not resolve it.

## PD-07 — Clean target for a multi-step write

**What's planted.** Opportunity `opp-demo-0088` (account `acct-demo-0008`, owner `rep-ashford`) is
in `Qualification` and has **no** seeded note and **no** seeded `meeting` activity.

**Expected handling.** Logging a meeting and then adding a note creates two new rows, and they are
the only rows that can satisfy the task's new-row assertions.

## Regeneration and verification

`scripts/verify_fixtures.sh` regenerates the full corpus from `fixtures/generate.py` into a scratch
directory and diffs every file against the committed `fixtures/*.json`, failing on any divergence.
All seven defects are covered by that byte-identical check, because they are applied
deterministically inside `generate_dataset()`; there is no separate defect-injection step.
